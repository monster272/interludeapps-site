How it works

Three decisions, then it runs itself.

No streak guilt, no shame screens, no counting the hours you have wasted. Just a door that asks something small of you before it opens.

1

Choose what to guard

Pick from your installed apps. Social, video and games float to the top, so you aren't scrolling past Calendar to find Instagram.

2

Set your practice times

The three sandhya hours come ready. Add your own, choose which days each repeats, and the lock arms itself — exactly, even while the phone sleeps.

3

Practise to open

Open a guarded app during a lock and the practice screen appears over it. Finish a sitting and every guarded app opens for as long as you chose.

Privacy

An app that watches your screen, and tells no one.

Sadhana Lock needs Android's accessibility service to know which app just came to the foreground. That is a serious permission, so here is exactly what it is allowed to do — every claim is checkable in the app's manifest.

  • ✓The service is declared unable to read window content. It cannot see your messages, your passwords or anything you type.
  • ✓It listens for one event — a window changing — and reads one thing: the package name of the app in front.
  • ✓An account is optional. The whole app works without one. Sign in with Google only if you want your practice carried to a new phone.
  • ✓The apps you guard never leave your phone. That list is the most revealing thing here, and it is excluded from the backup even when you are signed in.
  • ✓Anonymous diagnostics, and a switch to stop them. Crash reports and plain counts go to Firebase so we can catch phones that silently kill the lock. They never say which app you opened.
  • ✓No advertising, no data sold, and nothing shared with a third party for their own purposes.
