How it works

Three decisions, then it runs itself.

No streak guilt, no shame screens, no counting how many hours you have wasted. Just a door that asks something small of you before it opens.

1

Choose what to guard

Pick from your installed apps. The list puts the real time-sinks first — social, video, games — so you're not scrolling past Calendar to find Instagram.

2

Set your practice times

The three sandhya hours are offered by default. Add your own, choose which days each one repeats, and the lock arms itself — exactly, even while the phone sleeps.

3

Practise to open

When a guarded app is opened during a lock, the practice screen appears over it. Complete a sitting and every guarded app opens for as long as you chose.

The core loop

Three ways to sit. All of them count.

The unlock is never a puzzle, a maths problem or a punishment timer. It is a practice — and you choose which one you're in the mood for.

  • Guided. A shloka from the Gītā, in Devanagari with its meaning, rotating so you rarely meet the same one twice.
  • Your own. A breathing orb that expands and settles. Bring whatever practice is already yours.
  • Japa. A full 108-bead mala on screen, with a haptic tick per bead and a flourish at the guru bead.
  • An honest way out. Need maps, or to take a call? One tap opens that one app for ten minutes — not all of them. No begging, no countdown.

Privacy

An app that watches your screen, and tells no one.

Sadhana Lock needs Android's accessibility service to know which app just came to the foreground. That is a serious permission, so here is exactly what it is allowed to do — every claim below is checkable in the app's manifest.

  • The service is declared unable to read window content. It cannot see your messages, your passwords or anything you type.
  • It listens for one event — a window changing — and reads one thing: the package name of the app in front.
  • An account is optional. The whole app works without one. Sign in with Google only if you want your practice carried to a new phone.
  • The apps you guard never leave your phone. That list is the most revealing thing here, and it is excluded from the backup even when you are signed in. If you do sign in, your sittings, streak and reading positions are saved to your Google account so a new phone can find them — and deleting your account removes that copy.
  • Anonymous diagnostics, and a switch to stop them. Crash reports and plain counts — a lock appeared, a practice was completed — go to Google Firebase, so we can catch the phones that silently kill the lock. They never say which app you opened. One tap in Settings turns them off for good.
  • No advertising, no data sold, and nothing shared with a third party for their own purposes.
  • Your practice data is yours to take with you — save it to a file, restore it on a new phone.

Questions

The things people ask first.

Can I get around the lock if I really need to?

Yes, and deliberately so. "I need access now" opens the one app you were reaching for, for ten minutes. Not all of them — so needing maps in traffic doesn't hand you back Instagram for the afternoon. There's no shaming, no countdown and no limit on how often you use it. A lock you can't escape is a hostage situation, not a practice.

Do I have to be Hindu to use it?

No. The "your own practice" mode is a breathing orb with no words at all — bring whatever settles you. But the app doesn't hide what it is: the verses are from the Gītā and the Upaniṣads, and the japa mala is a real one.

Why does it need the accessibility permission?

It is the only way on Android to know which app has just come to the foreground. Sadhana Lock's service is declared unable to read window content and subscribes to a single event type, so all it ever learns is the package name of the app in front of you. Nothing is recorded and nothing is sent anywhere.

Will it keep working on my Realme, Oppo, Xiaomi or Vivo phone?

That's exactly the case it was built and tested for. Those skins shut down background apps aggressively, which silently breaks most blockers. Setup detects your skin and walks you through auto-launch and battery exemption; afterwards, the Lock Health screen tells you at a glance if anything has come loose.

Is my practice data safe if I change phones?

Yes. Android's own backup carries it across, and you can also save your practice history to a file and restore it yourself. A restore merges into what's already there — it never wipes what you've done.

Who wrote the translations?

We did. The Sanskrit and its romanisation come from public-domain sources and are left untouched; every English translation was written for this app, and the eight Indian-language translations were written from that English. Nothing is lifted from a copyrighted edition.

Is there an iPhone version?

Not today. iOS doesn't give apps the ability to notice another app opening the way this depends on, so an honest iPhone version would be a different product. Android only, for now.
